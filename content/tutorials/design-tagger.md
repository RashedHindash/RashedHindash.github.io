A character design sheet is not an illustration. It is a specification, and
everyone downstream reads it as one.

## What the video covers

The design of a stylised female character, recorded start to finish and sped up:
the costume, the hairstyle, the hero assets she carries, and the presentation
layout that holds them together. There is no commentary on the recording, so
what follows is the reasoning the video demonstrates but does not narrate.

## Stylisation is a decision about what to keep

Stylisation is usually described as a departure from realism, which is not quite
right. It is a decision about which parts of the anatomy to simplify and which
to leave alone, taken deliberately rather than uniformly.

Here the face carries the departure: the eyes are enlarged, the nose and jaw are
reduced, the surface is simplified to a few clean planes. The body stays much
closer to correct proportion, because it has to be modelled, rigged, and made to
walk and sit inside a world that also contains realistic props. Push the whole
figure equally and you get a cartoon. Push it selectively and you get a
stylised character who still occupies believable space.

## Costume is the fastest characterisation available

Before a character speaks or moves, the clothing has already told the audience
where she sits socially, how she expects to spend her day, and how much she can
afford to care about it.

The costume is assembled in legible layers: a cropped jacket over a plain crop
top, cargo trousers cuffed at the ankle, a utility belt with pouches at the hip,
a wallet chain, checkerboard sneakers. Each item is informal, mobile and
sub-cultural. The tension in the outfit is deliberate: a leg holster and belt
pouches read as equipment, but they are worn over streetwear rather than a
uniform, which places her outside any official structure.

The functional test is silhouette. Because the jacket is cropped and the top
ends above the waist, the waistline stays visible, so the figure keeps a clear
read at distance and at small scale. A longer jacket would have been easier to
draw and would have destroyed that.

## Hair is designed as a shape before it is designed as hair

Hairstyle decisions are silhouette decisions. A blunt bob with a straight fringe
gives the head a single stable shape that holds from the front, the side and
the back, which matters because animation will show all three and the modeller
has to build all three.

That is also why the back view exists on the sheet at all. Hair is one of the
few costume elements where the rear read is genuinely different information
rather than a mirror of the front.

## Hero assets carry the story the character cannot

Hero assets are the props with narrative weight: the backpack, the thigh
holster, the sidearm, the aviators, the lighters, the patches and pin badges.
They are drawn out separately, at larger scale, for two reasons that pull in
opposite directions. The modeller needs them legible enough to build, and the
audience will only ever see them small, so they must be designed to survive that
reduction.

The pins and patches are the cheapest characterisation on the sheet. A handful
of small graphics establish era, taste and allegiance without a line of
dialogue, and they cost almost nothing to produce or to change.

## The sheet itself is a designed object

Front and back views sit on a neutral ground at consistent scale under even
lighting, with the assets grouped in a column to the right behind a hard
divider. None of that is decoration.

The neutral background is there so the costume colours read true rather than
being tinted by a mood. The even lighting is there because this document
describes form and material, not atmosphere; the lighting decisions belong to
the shot, not to the sheet. The consistent scale lets anyone measure one part
against another. A presentation sheet that looks beautiful but cannot be
measured has failed at its actual job.

## What to watch for

- **Where the stylisation stops.** The face and the body are treated differently, and the boundary between them is the real design decision.
- **Silhouette before detail.** The outfit is resolved as a shape first, and the stitching, straps and buckles arrive only once that shape is settled.
- **The back view is not a mirror.** Watch what changes: the backpack, the hair mass, the holster straps.
- **Asset scale.** Each hero prop is drawn at a size that serves the modeller, not at the size it will appear on screen.
