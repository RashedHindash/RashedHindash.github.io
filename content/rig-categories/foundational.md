These are teaching rigs. Each one is deliberately impoverished — it can do less
than a character rig, and that is the entire point. What a beginner needs is not
more control but fewer places to hide.

The sequence matters more than any individual rig in it. Every stage adds
exactly one new problem while keeping everything already learned in play, so a
failure at any point is diagnosable: you know which variable was introduced last
and therefore where the animation broke. Working through them out of order, or
skipping ahead to the mannequin, produces the most common failure in
self-taught animation — a student who can produce a plausible walk cycle but
cannot say why a pose reads as weightless, because they never animated
something with no legs to distract from the timing.

## What each stage adds

**Ball** removes everything except when things happen and how far apart they
are. There is no anatomy, no appendage, no personality — only timing, spacing,
and the deformation that follows from speed. Almost every problem a student
will have three years from now is already present here in simplified form.

**Pendulum** fixes the base in space and hands over rotation. With translation
removed, overlap and drag become the only things capable of carrying the
motion, which is why they finally become visible.

**Ball and Tail** puts those two together: a translating base with something
attached that has to answer to it. This is the first rig where one part of the
object is required to disagree with another part, on purpose.

**Ball and Legs** introduces contact with the ground, and with it the whole
question of weight transfer. The moment a character supports itself, the
animator becomes responsible for where the mass is.

**Hammer** is about arcs and honest mass. A weighted head on a handle punishes
straight-line paths and uniform speed more obviously than anything else in the
set.

**Mannequin** removes the training wheels. Everything from the previous five is
still required, simultaneously, on a figure that has enough joints to twin
itself if you stop paying attention.

## How to use them

Animate the same exercise more than once rather than moving on when it works.
The second attempt is where the learning is, because the first is usually spent
finding out what the problem actually was.

Every rig here is free. Open the scene, and the controls are ready.
