Body mechanics asks what a character is doing. Acting asks why. That difference
sounds small and changes almost everything about how a shot is built.

In a mechanics shot the audience is reading force: where the mass is, what is
supporting it, how it is being transferred. The problems are physical and they
have physically correct answers. In an acting shot the audience is reading
intention, and intention has no correct answer, only a legible one. The work
shifts from making something obey physics to making something appear to be
thinking.

## Why these rigs have a face and a body

It is tempting to treat facial animation as a separate discipline bolted onto
the end of a shot. It is not, and rigs that split the two encourage the mistake.

A performance is distributed across the whole figure. The body establishes the
attitude and the weight of a moment before the face does anything, because
posture is visible at any distance and reads first. The face then supplies the
specificity: which of several possible emotions the posture is actually
carrying. When the two disagree, the audience believes the body, because
posture is much harder to fake and we are all extremely practised at reading it.

The practical consequence is an order of operations. Body first, always. Get the
weight and the line of action working while the face is still neutral. If a
performance is not readable in silhouette with a blank face, adding brows and
mouth shapes will not rescue it, it will only decorate the failure.

## What changes in the timing

Acting timing is slower and stranger than mechanics timing. Mechanics is
governed by physics, so the pacing has a floor: a body of a certain mass falls
at a certain rate. Performance has no such floor. A held moment can run far
longer than feels comfortable while animating and still read as too short when
watched.

Two things follow from that. The first is that stillness is a tool rather than a
gap: a character who stops moving is doing something, and the audience will read
meaning into it. The second is that changes of mind are the most valuable beats
available, because thought is only visible at the moment it changes direction.

## Using them

Both rigs here carry the same control set: squash and stretch with volume
retention, an IK/FK spine, and a full facial rig including brows, eyes and mouth
shapes. Emma adds slider control for the fingers.
