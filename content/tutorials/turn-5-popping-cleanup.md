The last video in the series deals with what is left: the pops, the small
hitches, and the final points of polish standing between the animation and a
clean overall read.

## What the video covers

Finding and removing pops wherever they have survived the earlier passes,
tidying anything the retiming and cleanup left behind, and taking the shot to a
state where the motion is smooth throughout.

## Why pops survive everything else

**A pop is a discontinuity, and discontinuities hide.** A single frame where a
value jumps is invisible while scrubbing and glaring at speed. They tend to
appear at the joins between passes, which is exactly where attention has already
moved on.

**They usually come from curves, not poses.** Most pops are a tangent doing
something abrupt, a duplicate key a frame apart, or a rotation crossing a
representational boundary. The pose either side is generally fine, which is why
looking at the poses does not find them.

**Cleanup is subtraction.** This pass should be removing problems rather than
adding ideas. If new animation is going in at this stage, something earlier was
not finished, and it is worth going back rather than layering over it.

## What to watch for

- **Play it at speed, repeatedly.** Pops are found by watching, not by scrubbing.
- **Duplicate and near-duplicate keys.** Two keys a frame apart with different values is a pop by construction.
- **Sudden large jumps in a rotation curve.** Usually a representation issue rather than a mistake in the animation.
- **Check the joins.** The frames where one pass handed over to the next are the likeliest place for something to have been left behind.
